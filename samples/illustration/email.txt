PORT BRIEF
Issue 12, Sunday 11 October 2026

Felixstowe congested as wind threatens London Gateway on Wednesday

Disruption likely: London Gateway and Hamburg. Congested: Felixstowe and Bremerhaven. Watch: Liverpool and Rotterdam. Rough seas in the Bay of Biscay may delay Asia, Mediterranean and West Africa services into the UK by 1–2 days. Reliability: Sea-Intelligence 49.9%, Xeneta 29%.

THE PORTS THIS WEEK

Felixstowe: Congested
Berths are 13 points busier than normal. Add 2–3 days to inland plans for containers arriving in the next 2 weeks, pre-book haulage and check your free time.

Southampton: Running normally
Running as expected with no predicted delays. Speak to your carrier about release and delivery now.

London Gateway: Disruption likely
Crane stoppages likely on Wednesday from 11:00 to 20:00, with gusts to 22 m/s. Tell your haulier to expect longer waits and allow 24–48 hours on collections.

Liverpool: Watch
Cranes may pause on Thursday morning. Allow 12–24 hours and avoid tight Thursday cut-offs.

Rotterdam: Watch
Berths are 7 points busier than usual. If you tranship here, watch berthing times and avoid tight feeder connections.

Antwerp: Running normally
Running as expected with no predicted delays. Speak to your carrier about release and delivery now.

Bremerhaven: Congested
Ships are spending 36% longer in port than usual. Add 2–3 days to inland plans for containers arriving in the next 2 weeks and pre-book haulage.

Hamburg: Disruption likely
A 24-hour strike is reported for Monday. Check your terminal's latest notice before booking collections and allow 24–48 hours.

ON THE WAY IN
Two storms matter for European arrivals. Biscay turns severe on Wednesday and Thursday, so services from Asia, the Mediterranean and West Africa into the UK are likely to run 1–2 days late. Ships rounding the Cape on Friday may lose up to 1 day. Don't book haulage against the published ETA.
